\section{Letter from the Chairs}

\begin{chairletter}{Farhan, Céline, and Yassine}
Dear Delegates,

Welcome to the World Trade Organization committee at IEUMUN 2026! We are honoured to serve as your chairs, and cannot wait to hear the arguments, alliances and solutions you will develop throughout this conference. As this is a beginner committee, we've designed both the topic and the pace of debate to be approachable regardless of your prior MUN experience, so don't let unfamiliarity hold you back from diving in.

The postwar trading system was built on a wager: that economic interdependence would make conflict too costly to pursue. That wager is now being tested like never before. From the collapse of the Appellate Body to the return of industrial policy, from semiconductor export controls to the invocation of Article XXI as a blank check, this committee sits at the fault line between two competing visions of the global economy. One vision is based on the idea that no state can unilaterally rewrite its own obligations; the other that sovereignty and security come first, rules be damned. We chose this topic because it forces us, and you, to recognize both truths at once: national security is a legitimate concern, but the exception built to protect it now threatens to swallow the rule itself.

This study guide is meant to be your starting point, not your finish line. We have laid out key terms, the history, the current situation, and bloc positions as fairly as we can, but this committee will only be as rich as the research you conduct beyond this document. We strongly encourage you to look into your country’s actual position, particularly regarding Article XXI, the Appellate Body crisis, and recent export control measures. The \emph{further reading} and \emph{bibliography} of this study guide can help you get started.

In committee this November, we hope to see rigorous debate and real collaboration. This is a technical topic, and one where genuine compromise is possible. We will be there to guide you through the days of Rules of Procedure (RoP), ensuring an orderly, respectful (and fun!) debate, and to help you build both your case and your confidence as delegates.

We look forward to meeting you all and seeing what we can build together.

\signoff[Your Chairs,]{Farhan, Céline, and Yassine}
\end{chairletter}
